How to Play

Clearing blocks

  • Click a block to select it, along with every block of the same color connected to it (up, down, left and right; not diagonally).
  • Click any of the selected blocks again to remove them all. While a group is selected, what it would score is shown next to your Board Score.
  • Changed your mind? Click anywhere else to clear the selection, then pick another group.
  • A group needs at least 2 blocks to be removed.
  • After a group is removed, the blocks above fall down to fill the gaps, then slide left.
  • When there are no groups of 2 or more left, the board is finished. Click Next Board to start a new one.

The Computer Player plays its own board next to yours, all by itself. On a phone, use the You | Computer switch above the board to watch it play.

Score

Every group you remove earns Score: its size times itself. So bigger groups are worth a lot more. One group of 10 is worth 100, but the same 10 blocks in five pairs are only worth 20.

Tip: pick a color to save. Clear the other colors first, so your saved color's blocks fall together into one big group, then take it all at once. Special blocks are worth saving for big groups too.

Special blocks

Once unlocked, grey special blocks appear on the board. When a group you remove touches one, it goes off and removes extra blocks, whatever their color. Those extra blocks add to your score.

  • +1 blocks remove every block touching your group. They add up: each +1 reaches one space further, so two +1s remove every block up to 2 spaces away from your group, three up to 3 spaces, and so on.
  • Line blocks (a white bar) remove every block in their row, if the bar is horizontal, or their column, if it's vertical.
  • Bomb blocks (a white ring) remove every block in the 3x3 square around them.
  • With the right Upgrades, some +1 blocks become +2 blocks (reaching 2 spaces), and some bombs become big bombs (two rings, clearing a 5x5 square).
  • Refill blocks (an arrow into a tray) fill the board back up: once the blocks have settled, new blocks drop in to fill every empty space. They can bring other special blocks with them, but never another refill block.
  • Color Blast blocks (a small rainbow ring) are rare. They remove every block on the whole board of the same color as your group, and it all counts as one group, so they're worth most when you've saved up a color.
  • They chain. If the area being removed reaches or touches another special block, that one goes off too, and the area grows again.
  • A special block can't be removed on its own. A single block touching one isn't enough either: you still need a group of 2.

Not sure what a move will do? Selecting a group highlights everything it would remove, before you commit.

Coins, Chips and Gems

Your currencies are shown at the top of the screen.

  • Coins: you earn 1 for every point of Score. Coins buy Upgrades for the Computer Player.
  • Chips: the Computer Player earns 1 for every point it scores. Chips buy Upgrades for you.
  • Gems are rarer, and buy the biggest Upgrades, for either player. You get Gems for:
    • each achievement (2 Gems, or more for the hardest)
    • finishing a board with a high enough board score. The goal goes up by 50 each time you reach it
    • finishing a board with 2 or fewer blocks left on it, and one more if there are none left
    • the Computer Player finishing its 10th board, then its 20th, 40th, 80th, and so on
    The next goals are shown above each board, as Gem at Board Score and Gem at Board #.

So playing yourself makes the Computer Player stronger, and leaving the Computer Player to play makes you stronger.

The Computer Player keeps playing even while the game is closed. It plays at full speed for the first 15 minutes, then slows down the longer you're away (an hour away is worth half an hour of play, and however long you're away, it's worth an hour at most). The Better While Away Upgrade, bought with Gems, makes it slow down less, so a night away can be worth almost 3 hours of play. When you come back, you'll see what it did while you were away.

Achievements and Augmentations

Achievements are things you accomplish while playing, like finishing your first board. Each one gives Gems, and some unlock an Augmentation: a new feature for you or for the Computer Player, such as +1 blocks. See the Achievements and Augmentations tabs.

Upgrades

Upgrades are bought one level at a time on the Upgrades tab, and each level costs more than the last. Everyday Upgrades cost Coins or Chips; game-changing ones cost Gems. Some Upgrades only become available once you've unlocked the Augmentation they improve. The Upgrades tab shows what each one does now, what the next level costs, and what you still need.
